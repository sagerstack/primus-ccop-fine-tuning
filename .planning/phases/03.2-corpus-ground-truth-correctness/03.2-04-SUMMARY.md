**1. [Rule 4 — Architectural] SC #9 re-classified from PASS target to N/A**

- **Found during:** Task 1 (B3-001 re-evaluation + corpus verification)
- **Issue:** The plan asserted `context_recall > 0` on B3-001 as the smoking-gun success gate for the corpus fix, on the assumption that pre-fix `context_recall=0` was caused entirely by section 5.3 missing from the index. Actual evidence:
  - Direct Qdrant scroll of `ccop_clauses_hybrid` confirms 2 section-5.3 points under `document_source='CCoP 2.0'`: `CCoP 2.0::5.3` (header) and `CCoP 2.0::5.3.1` (full PAM clause including MFA mandate)

  - Root cause is not corpus absence but retrieval ranking

- **Human-supplied funnel diagnostic (2026-04-21):**

  | Stage | Rank of CCoP 2.0::5.3.1 | Score |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hybrid retrieval (top_k=20, pre-rerank) | 11 / 20 | n/a |
  | Cross-encoder rerank | **5 / 20** | -6.773 |
  | Top-3 cutoff (rerank_top_n=3) | excluded | cutoff at rank 3 score -5.613 |

### For Phase 4

The following candidate fixes for the B3-001 `context_recall=0` regression are captured here so Phase 4 planning starts with a named option space:

1. **Bump `rerank_top_n` from 3 to 5** — cheapest fix. Would immediately surface `5.3.1` (rank 5) into the set consumed by RAGAs and the LLM. Trade-off: larger context window, potential for more noise in generation.
2. **Domain-tuned cross-encoder** — finetune on CCoP query/clause pairs so "shared admin accounts" ↔ "privileged accounts" synonymy is learned at the ranking layer. Trade-off: higher engineering cost, requires paired training data.
3. **Query rewriting / expansion** — lightweight pre-retrieval step that maps colloquial access-control vocabulary ("admin", "shared", "root") to CCoP's regulatory vocabulary ("privileged"). Trade-off: adds a pipeline stage; risk of expansion introducing drift.
