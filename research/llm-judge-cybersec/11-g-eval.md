# G-Eval (Liu et al., EMNLP 2023)

**Category**: Academic judge (reference-free NLG evaluator)
**Canonical source**: [Liu et al., 2023 — arXiv:2303.16634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634); ACL Anthology [2023.emnlp-main.153](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153/); 500+ citations by April 2026.

## Scoring mechanics

- **Backbone**: GPT-4 (strongest variant); GPT-3.5 tested as ablation.
- **Scale**: Discrete integer on a Likert-like scale (typical 1-5); framed as form-filling, not generation.
- **Composite**: Per-dimension scores; no fixed composite — evaluator returns dimension scores separately.
- **Probability-weighted score refinement**: Uses GPT-4 token log-probabilities at the score position to produce a continuous score, averaging candidate scores weighted by their probability. This is G-Eval's signature technical contribution — it dramatically reduces tie-heavy integer output into a real-valued score that correlates better with humans (reported Spearman 0.514 on summarization vs lower for prior methods).

## Prompt scaffolding

Three-stage prompt:

1. **Task Introduction**: defines the eval task (e.g., "evaluate summary for coherence").
2. **Evaluation Criteria**: dimension definition (e.g., "Coherence: collective quality of all sentences").
3. **CoT Evaluation Steps**: auto-generated by the LLM itself given the task + criteria — these become the chain-of-thought scaffold.
4. Evaluation applied to the (source, output) pair using a form-filling template returning a single integer.

Form-filling minimizes generation noise: the LLM cannot drift into commentary; it must emit the integer only.

## Ground-truth requirements

**Reference-free** — this is G-Eval's defining property. It requires:

- The source input (e.g., article to summarize).
- Criteria text (human-written or LLM-generated).
- No reference answer needed.

This makes G-Eval cheap to deploy on new datasets but unsuitable for our compliance use case as the sole judge: for regulatory QA, we need reference clauses and key facts — G-Eval's contribution is the scoring mechanics, not the reference-free property.

## Annotation cost

Minimal — only the dimension definitions need writing. In practice this is 5-15 lines of criteria text per dimension.

## Bias / reliability controls

- **Probability-weighted scoring**: replaces integer-tie-dominated output with a continuous score.
- **Auto-CoT**: reduces prompt-authoring variance by letting the LLM write the evaluation steps.
- Does NOT address: position bias (not pairwise), verbosity bias, self-enhancement bias, single-sample noise.

## Reported limitations (from paper)

- G-Eval exhibits **self-preference** — tends to rate GPT-generated text higher than human-written text on summarization, even when human is preferred by judges. Explicitly flagged by Liu et al.
- Human agreement (Spearman) is 0.514 on SummEval coherence — state of the art at publication but still far from human inter-annotator agreement (~0.7+).

## Citation/fact grounding

None — G-Eval does not decompose claims or verify citations. Coherence/fluency/relevance are judged holistically. For factuality, a separate metric (e.g., FActScore) is needed.

## Domain fit for cybersecurity compliance QA

- **Directly applicable elements**: (1) CoT auto-generation of evaluation steps for each dimension; (2) probability-weighted scoring to reduce tie artifacts in our 0-3 integer scale; (3) form-filling template design to prevent judge from drifting into commentary.
- **Not applicable as-is**: reference-free framing — regulatory QA requires anchoring to clauses; our ground truth is essential and must remain injected.
- **Concrete borrow**: Replace our single-integer-per-dimension output with a probability-weighted continuous score when the judge model exposes logprobs (Claude API does not; GPT-4o, open-source vLLM judges, and Prometheus 2 all do).
